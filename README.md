Al·ba·lu·mi·pho·bi·a
/ˌælbəˌluːmiˈfoʊbiə/
(al-bə-loo-mee-FOH-bee-uh)
noun
1. An intense aversion to or "fear" of light mode on digital screens, or bright digital screens; the physical discomfort caused by high-contrast white backgrounds and extreme display brightness.
2. The immediate urge to switch a device to dark mode upon encountering a bright user interface.

---

Nyc·to·lu·mi·pho·bi·a
/ˌnɪktoʊˌluːmiˈfoʊbiə/
(nik-toh-loo-mee-FOH-bee-uh)
noun
1. An intense aversion to or "fear" of dark mode; the discomfort caused by low-contrast interfaces or light-on-dark text.
2. The specific anxiety that using a dark screen will cause eye strain or make a device unusable in a bright room.

---

Al·ba·lu·mi·phil·i·a
/ˌælbəˌluːmiˈfɪliə/
(al-bə-loo-mee-FIL-ee-uh)
noun
1. A strong preference for "light mode" on digital devices.
2. The enjoyment of high-brightness, white-background interfaces, often citing better clarity or a "fresher" feel.

---

Nyc·to·lu·mi·phil·i·a
/ˌnɪktoʊˌluːmiˈfɪliə/
(nik-toh-loo-mee-FIL-ee-uh)
noun
1. A strong preference or "love" for dark mode; the state of finding comfort, focus, or aesthetic satisfaction in dark-themed digital interfaces.
2. The habit of immediately enabling "dark theme" on every new app or website encountered.
